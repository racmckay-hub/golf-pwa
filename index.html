📖 How to Use the Golf Swing Analyzer

🎯 Before You Start

The quality of your video directly affects the analysis accuracy.

  • ✅ Keep the original playback speed – Normal-speed and original slow-motion recordings are supported; avoid speeding up, slowing down, or re-timing the swing
  • ✅ Include one complete swing – Keep setup, impact, follow-through and finish; slow-motion clips may naturally be longer
  • ✅ Landscape video is required – Hold your phone horizontally, not vertically
  • ⚠️ Keep the full swing in frame – Do not crop the golfer, feet, hands, or club

🎯 What This Tool Does

Our AI-powered analyzer compares your golf swing to professional golfers and provides detailed feedback to help you improve.

📹 Video Requirements

  • Camera Position: Film from "Down the Line" (behind the golfer, facing the target)
  • Orientation: LANDSCAPE REQUIRED – hold your phone horizontally
  • Playback speed: Use the original recording speed. Original slow-motion is supported; do not convert it to normal speed or otherwise re-time the swing.
  • Trimming: You may remove unused footage before or after the swing, but keep the complete swing sequence intact.
  • Full Body: Ensure your entire body and the club are visible
  • Lighting: Use good lighting (outdoor or bright indoor)
  • Frame Rate: 30fps or 60fps recommended
  • Format: MP4, MOV, or AVI (max 100MB)

✅ Example of a Good Video:

  • Landscape (wide) orientation, with your full body and club visible
  • Original playback speed preserved (normal speed or original slow motion)
  • Complete swing visible from setup through finish
  • Full body visible from head to toes
  • Club visible throughout the swing
  • Good lighting, no shadows on the golfer
  • Camera at waist height, behind the golfer

📊 What You'll Get

  • Overall Score: 0-100 rating of your swing
  • Key Metrics: Spine angle, hip rotation, X-factor, and more
  • Pro Comparison: See how you compare to Rory McIlroy, Tiger Woods, and others

  • Progress Tracking: Review saved scores and metric changes over time
  • Before & After: Compare two completed analyses metric by metric
  • Personalized Recommendations: Specific drills and swing thoughts

Understand Your Swing Metrics

↔️X-FactorTop · Degrees

What it measures: The separation between shoulder rotation and hip rotation at the top of the backswing.

Why it matters: It describes how the upper and lower body coil relative to one another. More is not automatically better; mobility, control and the selected professional's pattern matter.

🏌️Club Angle at TopTop · Degrees

What it measures: The detected shaft angle relative to the image horizontal at the top of the backswing.

How to read it: A higher value is more vertical in the video frame; a lower value is flatter. Camera position and clear club visibility are important.

🔄Hip Rotation at TopTop · Degrees

What it measures: How far the hip line has turned from its address reference by the top of the backswing.

Why it matters: It helps explain backswing depth, coil and how the pelvis supports the upper-body turn.

🧭Shoulder RotationTop · Degrees

What it measures: How far the shoulder line turns from address to the top of the backswing.

How to read it: Interpret it together with hip rotation and X-Factor rather than as an isolated target.

💪Lead Arm Angle at TopTop · Degrees

What it measures: The position of the lead arm at the top, calculated from the lead shoulder, elbow and wrist landmarks.

Why it matters: It helps describe arm depth and structure. Handedness is applied so the correct lead side is measured.

🦾Lead Elbow AngleTop · Degrees

What it measures: The joint angle formed by the lead shoulder, elbow and wrist.

How to read it: A larger angle generally indicates a straighter lead arm; compare with your chosen professional rather than chasing a universal number.

🦾Trail Elbow AngleImpact · Degrees

What it measures: The joint angle formed by the trail shoulder, elbow and wrist around impact.

Why it matters: It describes how the trail arm is delivering and extending through the strike.

⭕Hip RotationImpact · Degrees

What it measures: Pelvis rotation relative to address at impact.

Why it matters: It shows how open the lower body is at the strike and should be interpreted with torso rotation, mobility and camera alignment.

🎯Shoulder Rotation at ImpactImpact · Degrees

What it measures: Shoulder-line rotation from address to the impact frame.

Why it matters: Comparing shoulder and hip rotation helps describe body sequence and delivery position.

📐Spine AngleAddress · Degrees

What it measures: Torso tilt from vertical at address, using the midpoint of the shoulders and hips.

Why it matters: It describes setup posture. The goal is a stable, athletic position that suits your body and club—not one universal angle.

🦵Knee FlexionAddress · Degrees

What it measures: The average bend of both knees using hip, knee and ankle landmarks.

Why it matters: It indicates how athletic and balanced the setup is. Full visibility of the legs and feet improves reliability.

🧍Posture ScoreAddress · Score

What it measures: A combined setup-quality score derived from posture-related measurements rather than a single body angle.

How to read it: Use it as a summary indicator, then inspect spine angle and knee flexion to understand what drove the score.

↔Lateral MovementFull swing · Percent

What it measures: Side-to-side movement of the estimated body center relative to the frame.

Why it matters: It helps identify excessive sway or insufficient pressure shift. Camera movement can distort this metric, so keep the phone fixed.

⏱️Tempo RatioFull swing · Ratio

What it measures: Backswing duration divided by downswing duration.

How to read it: A value of 3.0 means the backswing took three times as long as the downswing. Original slow-motion footage is supported, but edited, variable-speed, or re-timed playback can make tempo unreliable and may cause it to be excluded.
